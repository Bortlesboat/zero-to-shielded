01

Pick a wallet

~3 min

Privacy only works if your wallet can hold shielded ZEC. Many popular multi-coin wallets only support transparent addresses, which are public like Bitcoin. Use a wallet built for shielded Zcash.

Zodl (formerly Zashi)

  1. Install Zodl from the App Store or Google Play. It's also on F-Droid. Check that the publisher matches the link on zodl.com.
  2. Tap Create new wallet.
  3. That's it. The wallet is shielded by default and supports in-app swaps.

Why this one for beginners: it defaults to shielded, shields with one tap, and lets you swap other coins into shielded ZEC without an exchange account.

Zingo!, Zkool, Cake, and others

  1. Open the ZecHub wallet list and filter for shielded / Ironwood-ready desktop wallets.
  2. Download only from the link the project itself publishes.
  3. Create a new wallet. The screens will look different from the phone tab, but the steps are the same.

Keystone or Ledger + a companion app

  1. Keystone pairs with Zodl for shielded cold storage. Ledger added shielded Zcash signing in 2026.
  2. Set up the device first. Its screen is where you'll confirm every send.
  3. Great for larger amounts. For a first try, a phone wallet is fine.

Avoid for now: exchange accounts ("not your keys") and transparent-only wallets. Coinbase-style balances and multi-coin wallets like Exodus or Trust can't hold shielded ZEC.

What the world sees

Nothing. Creating a wallet happens entirely on your device. Nothing is written to the blockchain, and nobody learns you have one.

02

Back up your recovery phrase

~3 min

Your recovery phrase is your money. If your phone dies, those words plus your wallet's birthday height bring everything back. If anyone else sees them, they can take everything.

  1. In the wallet, open Settings → Recovery phrase (or follow the backup prompt).
  2. Write all the words on paper, in order. No screenshots, cloud notes, or password-manager "secure notes" synced to email.
  3. Also write down the wallet birthday height. It's a block number that tells the wallet where to start scanning, so a restore takes minutes instead of hours.
  4. Store the paper somewhere safe, and consider a second copy in another location.

Scam alert: no wallet, support agent, or "Ironwood migration" site will ever need your phrase. Anyone who asks for it is stealing.

03

Know your addresses

~2 min

Zcash has public (transparent) addresses and private (shielded) ones. Your wallet shows both under Receive. Share the shielded one whenever you can. Paste any address below to see what kind it is.

Checks the prefix and checksum right in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Cheat sheet

u1… Unified address. The default in modern wallets. It bundles a shielded receiver and sometimes a transparent one, and the sender's wallet picks the most private option.

zs1… Sapling address. Older, still shielded, still works.

t1… / t3… Transparent address. Fully public, like Bitcoin. Some exchanges only support these.

tex1… TEX address. Can only receive from transparent funds. Good wallets unshield for you automatically in two steps.

04

Get some ZEC

~5 min

Start small. A few dollars is enough to learn with. There are three common ways in.

  1. In Zodl, open Swap. It's powered by NEAR Intents.
  2. Choose what you're paying with (for example BTC, ETH, SOL, or a stablecoin). You'll get a deposit address.
  3. Send that coin from wherever you hold it. Shielded ZEC arrives in your wallet.

Nice bonus: this lands directly in your shielded balance, so you can skip step 5.

  1. Buy ZEC on an exchange that lists it.
  2. Choose Withdraw and paste your wallet's address. If the exchange rejects a u1… address, use the transparent t1… one from your wallet instead.
  3. Send a small test amount first, then the rest.

Landed as transparent? Totally normal. That's what step 5 is for.

  1. Send your friend your shielded (u1…) address.
  2. Ask them to include a memo. Only you will be able to read it.
  3. It arrives shielded. Nobody else can see who sent it, how much, or the memo.

Patience: a block arrives about every 75 seconds. Funds show as pending until they have a few confirmations, which usually takes a few minutes.

What the world sees

Exchange → your t1 address: everything. The exchange knows it's you, and anyone can see that address's balance.

Swap or friend → your shielded address: the incoming side is private. The chain shows that a shielded transaction happened, and nothing about who sent it or how much.

05

Shield it

~1 min

Shielding moves ZEC from your public (transparent) balance into your private (shielded) balance. From then on, spending it reveals nothing.

  1. If your wallet shows a transparent balance, tap Shield. Zodl offers this with one tap.
  2. Confirm. The fee is tiny, around 0.0001 ZEC.
  3. Wait for confirmation. Your shielded balance goes up and your transparent balance goes to zero.

Privacy habit: don't shield and then immediately unshield the exact same amount. Matching amounts in and out are easy to link. Leave funds shielded and spend them shielded.

What the world sees

Shielding is visible on the way in: "t1… sent 1.0 ZEC into the shielded pool." After that, the trail goes cold. Nobody can see where those coins go next.

06

Send & receive privately

~2 min

This is the moment: your first fully shielded transaction. Send some to a friend, or to a second wallet you control.

  1. Tap Send and paste a shielded address (u1… or zs1…).
  2. Enter an amount. Add a memo if you like, such as "thanks for lunch 🍜". It's encrypted, and only the recipient can read it.
  3. Review and confirm. Double-check the first and last few characters of the address.
  4. To receive, share your u1… address or its QR code. You can share it as often as you like, and wallets can create fresh ones too.

No undo: transactions are final. For anything big, send a small test amount first.

What the world sees

A shielded transaction happened, and the fee it paid. sender recipient amount memo are all encrypted. Only you and the recipient can see them.

07

Unshield (only when you need to)

~2 min

Sometimes you need to pay a transparent address, for example to deposit at an exchange. Sending from your shielded balance to a t1… or tex1… address is called unshielding.

  1. Tap Send and paste the transparent address. Your wallet will note that this amount becomes public.
  2. Send only what you need. Your remaining balance stays shielded.
  3. For tex1… addresses (used by some exchanges), good wallets handle the required two-step route for you.

Privacy habit: avoid unshielding the exact amount you just shielded, right after shielding it. Different amounts and some time in between make that link much harder to draw.

What the world sees

"The shielded pool paid 0.5 ZEC to t1…." The amount and destination are public. Where it came from inside the pool is not.

Held shielded ZEC before July 28, 2026? The Ironwood network upgrade (NU6.3) replaced the Orchard pool with a new shielded pool. Orchard now only allows withdrawals. Updated wallets offer to move your funds for you. Your addresses stay the same, and Sapling and transparent ZEC remain spendable. Only use the migration built into your wallet, and never a website that asks for your phrase.

HELP

Stuck?

My balance says "pending" or "syncing".

Wallets scan the chain privately on your device, so a first sync can take a few minutes. New funds become spendable after a few confirmations, at about 75 seconds per block. Keep the app open and on Wi-Fi.

My ZEC arrived as "transparent".

The sender used your t1 address, which is common with exchanges. Tap Shield (step 5).

The exchange won't accept my address.

Some exchanges only accept transparent addresses. Copy the transparent address from your wallet's Receive screen for deposits into your wallet, then shield it.

I restored my wallet and my balance is missing.

Enter the correct birthday height, or an earlier one, and let it rescan. A birthday later than your first transaction will skip your funds.

Someone says I must "migrate to Ironwood" on their website.

Scam. Migration happens inside your wallet app. No legitimate process asks for your recovery phrase.

How private is this, really?

Fully shielded transactions hide sender, receiver, amount, and memo on-chain. Privacy leaks at the edges: shielding from and unshielding to transparent addresses, matching amounts and timing, and network metadata (some wallets offer Tor). Staying shielded is the strongest default.
